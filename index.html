      How to study for the Einbürgerungstest

      The test in 30 seconds

      • The test has 33 multiple-choice questions, each with four answers and one correct one.
      • You get 60 minutes and need at least 17 correct to pass.
      • 30 questions come from the general pool of 300 that applies in every federal state. 3 come from your state's pool, here the 10 Bavaria questions.
      • Every possible question is in this trainer. The exam only reuses them, so learning all 310 is enough.

      A simple routine

      1. Study every day. It shows what is due for review first, then 15 new questions. Clear the due ones before moving on.
      2. Learn the 10 Bavaria questions in the first days. They come first among the new ones.
      3. Take a Mock exam about once a week, and daily in the last week. Aim for 27 or more out of 33 so you have a safety margin over 17.
      4. With around 15 new questions a day you will have seen everything in about three weeks. Keep reviewing until the exam.

      What the tabs do

      📖 Study
      Your daily session. Due questions first (weakest first), then up to 15 new ones. A question you miss comes back once at the end of the session.
      🎯 Mock exam
      33 random questions in 60 minutes, like the real test. You can change answers until you press Finish exam, and the clock keeps running if you reload. When it hits zero the exam is submitted. Afterwards you can step through every question with the explanation.
      📚 Browse…
      Filters for looking things up: all questions in catalogue order, general only, Bavaria only, questions you have not seen yet, your mistakes, and the ones you have mastered.

      Tips for learning

      • The exam is in German. Start with the English translations switched on, then switch them off (the 🌐 button) once the German wording feels familiar.
      • After a wrong answer, read the Why note. Understanding the reason sticks better than memorising the letter.
      • Watch for “nicht” in questions such as “Which is not …”. They ask for the exception.
      • Dates, numbers and names (1945, 1949, 1961, 1989, 1990, 16 states, 5% threshold) come up a lot. Make small flash cards for them.
      • Some questions show a picture: coats of arms, a map, a ballot paper or photographs. Look at the image before the answers.
      • Short sessions every day work better than one long session a week.

      Good to know

      • This is an unofficial study aid. The German wording is what counts in the exam. The English translations and explanations are there to help you understand.
      • The questions come from the BAMF catalogue dated 07.05.2025. A few answers depend on the date, such as the current Chancellor, the largest parliamentary groups and the population. Check them against the latest catalogue before your exam.
      • Details about registering for the test and where to take it are handled by your local authority or test centre. This trainer does not cover them.
